## The idea

For a while now I have had an idea in my head: to start doing **IT consulting**.

It is not an escape and not a fallback. It is the desire to build something of my own: to put the skills gathered over years of work — Linux infrastructures, hosting platforms, virtualization, automation, security — at the service of more projects and more people, instead of selling them to a single employer.

There is also a question of **time**. Today my time is, in practice, decided by others: schedules, priorities, urgencies. Consulting, in my mind, is the chance to manage it myself — to choose the projects, to decide how much and when to work, and to see the value of what I do directly. And, of course, to earn a living on my own terms.

## What is holding me back

If it were simple, I would have done it already. The reasons why I am still at the starting line are five, and all very concrete.

**I have no clients yet.** It sounds trivial, but that is the point: a consulting business exists the moment someone pays you to solve a problem. Until that "someone" shows up, there is only an idea — and ideas alone do not pay the bills.

**I am not a good "salesman".** I am a technician. I am comfortable with systems, networks, problems to diagnose and solutions to build; much less with selling myself, networking, talking about myself. And yet consulting is, for a good half of it, exactly that.

**I cannot leave my job hoping to find clients.** This is the most serious reason. I have responsibilities and people who rely on me: quitting a stable job to "see how it goes" is not a bet I can afford. Financial security is not a luxury you start from: it is the ground that lets you take a risk.

**There is Italian bureaucracy.** Setting up a VAT number, choosing a tax regime, paying social contributions, keeping the books, meeting deadlines: a world of formalities that in Italy weighs more than elsewhere. It is not an insurmountable obstacle — plenty of people do it — but it is a real barrier, made also of fixed costs that arrive in the months when you invoice nothing. Before starting you need to know it well, not discover it along the way.

**And salaried employment has its advantages anyway.** I do not want to tell myself the fairy tale of independence at all costs: a salary at the end of the month, paid holidays, sick leave, contributions paid, training, colleagues to compare notes with — and the chance to genuinely switch off when you stop. Becoming a consultant means giving up all those safety nets: income becomes variable, taxes and contributions become a constant worry, and work never quite leaves you. It is a change of life, not just of contract: better to be aware of it.

## How I plan to start

The answer, on reflection, is only one: **start as a side job**.

Keep the job and build the consulting practice in the time that remains, calmly, without turning life upside down.

1. **Start small** — a few projects, a few clients, without giving anything up.
2. **Understand whether it works** — whether there is demand for what I can do, whether I can handle clients, quotes, deadlines and expectations, whether I actually enjoy it.
3. **Decide only later** — when (and if) the workflow becomes steady, then it will make sense to talk about a change of life.

I am in no hurry. I would rather build something solid in two years than burn out in two months.

## In the meantime

While this idea matures, I am also looking elsewhere: there are other fields that intrigue me and that I would like to explore, so as not to put all my eggs in one basket. I will write about that in a separate article.
